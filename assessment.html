Hays County example · Evidence before estimates

Waterleaf Park: What can we actually prove?

A real City of Kyle native-meadow restoration project used to demonstrate scientific evidence standards. This is a documented-activity case study, not a claim that restoration benefits or cost savings have already been measured.

Evidence we can trace: The City's August 2025 report documents restoration planning on five assessed acres. Its December 2025 report documents invasive King Ranch bluestem treatment and no-till planting of a native seed mixture. These are reported activities—not independently measured ecological outcomes.

How this becomes a credible Hays County assessment

  1. Define the same treatment area. Obtain a releasable GIS polygon, and reconcile 5 acres assessed, 5.01 acres internally logged as seeded and 3.6 acres in the promotional example. Do not assume their boundaries are identical.
  2. Measure starting conditions. Record vegetation cover, soil and compaction, rainfall, native/exotic species, management and baseline expenses.
  3. Use comparable methods. Run an appropriate hydrologic model for existing and proposed conditions; keep model files, parameters, uncertainties and a reviewer sign-off. EPA's Stormwater Calculator is a screening tool, not a native-prairie hydrology guarantee.
  4. Validate the follow-up. Repeat vegetation and wildlife surveys over matched seasons. Record establishment, failed areas, invasive maintenance and site condition.
  5. Publish the actual difference. Separately report auditable operating costs, modeled physical outcomes and qualified nonmarket monetary benefits. No double-counting or unverified return-on-investment claim.
