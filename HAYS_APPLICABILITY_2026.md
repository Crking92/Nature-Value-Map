# Hays County site-applicability evidence — October 2026

## Decision
The Nature Value Map publishes **conditional study benchmarks**, not measurements of any individual Hays County parcel. Site context is user-reported and is not independently verified by the map. The site context never changes a published rate. If the user identifies an engineered drainage site but selects natural wetland or riparian forest, the incompatible dollar reference is withheld. See [SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-29.md](SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-29.md) for original source values and their monetary years.

## Why Hays County needs separate site categories
- **Blackland Prairie:** Clay-rich soils and prairie vegetation, generally toward eastern Hays County. USGS Texas ecoregions and TPWD vegetation layers supply regional context, not parcel-level condition.
- **Edwards Plateau:** Commonly limestone/karst terrain, shallow or variable soils and different drainage and vegetation. A prairie benefit-transfer coefficient cannot be assumed equally accurate here.
- **Engineered stormwater swale or detention basin:** A constructed basin is not automatically a functional wetland, natural riparian corridor or prairie. Hydrology, vegetation and soils need on-site examination.
- **Creek corridor:** Woody riparian cover, floodplain connectivity and channel condition need confirmation before using the national FEMA riparian benchmark.
- **Confirmed inland wetland:** Confirmation of wetland conditions does not make statewide wetland averages site-specific.

## Local ecological research

**Hutchinson, Maroti, Kapoor & Dessouky (2026)**, *Establishment of native graminoids in roadside swales dominated by non-native grasses in Central Texas*, Urban Forestry & Urban Greening 119, article 129373. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ufug.2026.129373. Eight native graminoids were evaluated over two growing seasons in Central Texas swales near the Edwards Aquifer recharge zone. Survival depended on species, soil compaction, drought and hydroperiod. Findings are relevant to landscape management; they do **not** provide a general annual dollar value, measured Hays County runoff coefficient or universal species survival rate.

**Hutchinson, Clearwater, Kent, Kapoor & Dessouky (2026)**, *Spontaneous vegetation diversity in managed stormwater swales and detention basins in the Edwards Aquifer recharge zone of Central Texas*, Journal of Environmental Management 406, article 129841. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2026.129841. The researchers recorded 115 species (89 native) across sampled swales and 94 species (68 native) across sampled detention basins. These are aggregated research observations, not counts predicted for user-selected areas. Facultative and upland vegetation were common, with few obligate wetland species.

## Data needed to advance a benchmark toward a local estimate

Document: mapped acreage and location; observed land cover, abundance and site history; soils/compaction and slope; watershed/hydrology; restoration age, planting composition and survivorship; maintenance record and costs by year; climate/weather; and a compatible, documented model with uncertainty. For monetary claims, preserve source geography, service definitions and dollar year, prevent double counting, and keep restoration expenditures distinct from nonmarket ecosystem-service benefits.

**Publication rule:** Keep published benchmark figures clearly qualified. Never assign the wetland valuation simply because a property contains a detention pond. Do not assign the EcoMetrics non-native right-of-way dollar value to manicured turf. Do not create a universal carbon, infiltration or biodiversity result from the parcel area alone.
