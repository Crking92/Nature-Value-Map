# Nature Value Map — Carbon evidence and model eligibility protocol

**Status:** Scientific review plan only, **not** a calibrated carbon calculator or credit estimator. No new carbon default appears in Nature Value Map.

## Separate the endpoints

- **Carbon stock** means the mass stored in soil or woody/herbaceous biomass at a given date, commonly reported as Mg C/ha or tonnes C/ac. It is **not automatically annual sequestration**.
- **Change in carbon stock** requires at least two comparable stock measurements separated by a documented period; it must account for soil depth, bulk density, stones/coarse fragments, sampled area, season, and management.
- **Greenhouse gas flux / net change** is a different endpoint and may include CO₂, CH₄ and N₂O in **tonnes CO₂-equivalent over time**. For biological systems, one cannot treat an increase in soil carbon stock as an entire life-cycle GHG benefit without accounting for other emissions and baseline.
- **Avoided emissions** and **carbon removals** should be reported separately where methods allow.

## Appropriate sources

1. USDA NRCS [Soil Health Testing and Soil Organic Carbon Stock Monitoring (CEMA 221)](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/conservation-basics/soil/soil-health/soil-health-testing). Uses actual before/after sampling, known sampling depth, measured carbon and bulk density, and repeated observation over multiple years. Its protocols are designed for specified agricultural contexts, not automatically every municipal landscaped plot.
2. USDA/Colorado State [COMET-Planner](https://comet-planner.com/) provides **generalized practice-based planning estimates** for relevant NRCS conservation practices, not site-measured carbon gains. Its version and coefficients need recording; practice applicability must be checked.
3. [COMET-Farm](https://toolkit.climate.gov/tool/comet-farm) enables more site-specific agricultural GHG scenario analysis with management history, but it is also a **model**, not proof of measured flux. Its land-use/practice parameters cannot be assumed valid for a native city park or lawn-to-wildflower conversion.
4. [EPA Guidelines for Preparing Economic Analyses (2024)](https://www.epa.gov/environmental-economics/guidelines-preparing-economic-analyses-3rd-edition) for counterfactual, timing, uncertainty and monetary claims.

## Minimum site metadata for future quantification

- Actual site polygon/area, baseline and treatment land-cover types, management history, planting/seed source, treatment date and observed establishment;
- Soil series/ecological site, sample date, georeferenced locations, depth interval, lab method, dry bulk density, coarse fragment fraction, soil C concentration, repeat sampling design;
- Woody biomass measurement method where applicable; whether grassland soil C and biomass C are represented separately;
- Baseline and post-treatment management including fertilizer, irrigation, mowing and fuel if greenhouse gas accounting is attempted;
- Model name/version, variables, confidence/sensitivity assessment and original output files; reviewer sign-off.

## Publication gate

- **Not ready**: fixed tonnes CO₂e/acre/year for any native meadow or roadside conversion, or dollar-valued carbon credits from land area alone.
- **Potentially ready as a contextual reference**: published peer-reviewed or USDA-model estimate for the **specific appropriate practice and reference ecosystem** with all applicability qualifiers and source year.
- **Potentially ready as a site output**: an externally documented and scientifically reviewed model or standardized baseline-and-follow-up field measurements. Separate stocks, annual flux, economic value and uncertainty.

No universal carbon monetary figure will be inserted into the ecology dollar total; existing source breakdowns may already include climate-related benefits and must be reviewed for overlap.
