What this comparison can—and cannot—tell you

Mowing differences are not the same as total savings. Native habitat still requires establishment, invasive-plant management, inspections, and sometimes selective mowing.

This version does not sum ecological benefit-transfer dollars and your avoided mowing costs into a single “net value.” They describe different types of benefits and have different assumptions and dollar years.

Ten-year comparison = 10 × (current annual mowing − proposed annual mowing − proposed annual restoration management) − one-time restoration installation. No discounting, inflation, land acquisition, irrigation, or additional costs unless separately modeled.

Peer-reviewed · 2023

Lawn versus meadow: real ecological trade-offs

Paudel and States reviewed biodiversity, infiltration, aesthetics, recreation and other ecosystem services and disservices of grassy lawns and floral meadows. The review does not supply a universal Hays County dollar-per-acre lawn value.

Peer-reviewed · Corn Belt · 2020 USD

What has lawn-to-pollinator conversion cost elsewhere?

A 10-year modeled study reported $54–$167/ac/year annualized for converted pollinator habitat, versus $326–$1,034/ac/year for hired lawn care. It used a 2% discount rate, Midwestern assumptions and specific establishment practices—not local quotes.

Read Janke, Tyndall & Bradbury (2021)

Sources, assumptions and limitations

How calculations work

Acres × published reference $/acre/year for eligible land-cover references only. Habitat questions are descriptive, not numeric multipliers.

Mixed-cover references are calculated only when percentages total 100%, land-cover zones do not overlap, and every selected reference is eligible and expressed in 2021 dollars.

All economic valuations are transfers from studies—not measurements of the mapped site. Dollar figures from different studies are not averaged automatically.
