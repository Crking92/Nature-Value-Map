What this comparison can—and cannot—tell you

Mowing differences are not the same as total savings. Native habitat still requires establishment, invasive-plant management, inspections, and sometimes selective mowing.

This version does not sum ecological benefit-transfer dollars and your avoided mowing costs into a single “net value.” They describe different types of benefits and have different assumptions and dollar years.

Ten-year illustration = 10 × (current annual mowing − proposed annual mowing − other restoration management) − one-time restoration installation. Enter non-mowing management separately so the same mowing cost is not subtracted twice. Before using the result in a financial decision, normalize inputs to one price year and perform a discounted analysis; this version has no discounting, inflation, or allowance for other omitted costs.

Research beyond dollar totals

What do comparisons actually show?

Different studies answer different questions. These findings are not inputs to the map's ecosystem-dollar reference, and they are not added together.

Peer-reviewed · 2023

Lawn versus meadow: real ecological trade-offs

Paudel and States reviewed biodiversity, infiltration, aesthetics, recreation and other ecosystem services and disservices of grassy lawns and floral meadows. The review does not supply a universal Hays County dollar-per-acre lawn value.

Read the peer-reviewed review

Peer-reviewed · Corn Belt · 2020 USD

What has lawn-to-pollinator conversion cost elsewhere?

A 10-year modeled study reported $54–$167/ac/year annualized for converted pollinator habitat, versus $326–$1,034/ac/year for hired lawn care. It used a 2% discount rate, Midwestern assumptions and specific establishment practices—not local quotes.

Read Janke, Tyndall & Bradbury (2021)

Estimate water effects with actual site inputs

EPA's National Stormwater Calculator models annual runoff using land cover, soil, rainfall and other site conditions. EPA describes hydrology analysis for sites under 12 acres; larger or complex sites may need a different model.

Peer-reviewed · Central Texas · 2026

Engineered swales aren't automatically wetlands

A Central Texas study documented native and non-native vegetation in roadside swales and detention basins in the Edwards Aquifer recharge area. These sites often contained upland and facultative vegetation, with few obligate wetland plants. Its findings support field assessment—not assigning the $18,948 wetland benchmark to every basin.

Read Hutchinson et al. (2026)

Government technical cost scenario · Texas FY2025

Statewide native range-planting cost: $225.38 per acre

NRCS Practice 550, Scenario 4, models a 150-acre predominantly native range-planting project with standard seedbed preparation and a 75%-native minimum. This is not a Kyle parks quote, and it does not meet a strict 100% local-native plant specification.

Sources, assumptions and limitations

How calculations work

Acres × published reference $/acre/year for eligible land-cover references only. Habitat questions are descriptive, not numeric multipliers.

Mixed-cover references are calculated only when percentages total 100%, land-cover zones do not overlap, and every selected reference is eligible and expressed in 2021 dollars.

All economic valuations are transfers from studies—not measurements of the mapped site. Dollar figures from different studies are not averaged automatically.
